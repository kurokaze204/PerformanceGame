# River Diagram mechanic

This simulation uses the River Diagram concept developed by Geoff Parcell and Chris Collison and described in *No More Consultants: We Know More Than We Think*. The tool is intended to make internal strengths, weaknesses and opportunities for peer learning visible before an organisation assumes it must buy outside expertise.

## Game representation

The five knowledge domains form the X axis. Knowledge score forms the Y axis. Each active site is plotted as a three-letter point label using its strongest locally available score in that domain: the higher of Team Capability and Local Codified Knowledge.

For each domain:

- the **north bank** is the highest site score;
- the **south bank** is the lowest site score;
- the blue **river** is the gap between them.

The chart is deliberately available throughout play so teams can use it as a strategy and discussion tool, not merely as an end-of-game report.

## Knowledge Sharing intervention

The River Diagram includes a targeted site-to-site **Knowledge Sharing** action during Invest.

1. Choose a domain.
2. Choose a stronger teaching site.
3. Choose a receiving site.
4. The receiving site's Team Capability is lifted to approximately **80% of the teaching site's current local knowledge score**, rounded to the nearest whole knowledge point and capped at 6.
5. The action costs **1 Action + $18k**, attributed to the receiving site.

This mechanic is intentionally more powerful and targeted than Corporate Intranet publication. Site-to-site sharing represents richer peer learning and transfer of practice; the Intranet spreads reusable knowledge more broadly but in a more generic form.

## Learning intent

The River Diagram should help players recognise that:

- capability can already exist somewhere inside the organisation while remaining inaccessible elsewhere;
- the best internal source is often identifiable;
- KM can deliberately connect strong and weak parts of the organisation;
- consultants or external expertise are most defensible for knowledge beyond the organisation's current north bank, rather than as the first response to an internal sharing problem.

## Playtest questions

- Is the 80% transfer rule strong enough to make peer sharing visibly valuable without collapsing the river too quickly?
- Should the teaching score remain `max(Team, Codified)`, or should later versions distinguish practice-based and codified transfer?
- Is $18k appropriate relative to the Corporate Intranet and Corporate Training costs?
- Do players begin using the River Diagram proactively before selecting interventions?
- Does the diagram make the distinction between *knowledge exists* and *knowledge is accessible* clearer?
